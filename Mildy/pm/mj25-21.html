Weak topics to target next session

Surds and roots

Question 8(b) reveals a genuine misconception: a square root cannot be distributed across a sum, since √(a+b) ≠ √a + √b. Squaring both sides is the reliable method in such cases. Additional practice on isolating and squaring expressions correctly would help reinforce this.

Probability: replacement and "exactly one"

Two separate errors in Question 17 point to the same underlying gap: not tracking what changes, or does not change, between draws, and confusing "both" with "exactly one." It would be worthwhile to review the distinction between sampling with and without replacement, and to practise constructing "exactly one of two" problems using the method (A, B) + (B, A).

Multi-step "show that" questions

Each blank answer (Q18c, Q18e, Q21b) involved a multi-step derivation worth several marks; these were not incorrect attempts, but non-attempts. This is consistent with previous observations that confidence can drop when a question requires several linked steps. Breaking similar questions into smaller, guided steps, and building confidence gradually with past-paper "show that" questions, should help directly.

Handwriting clarity

Marks were lost on Q10d purely because the digit written was not legible (it could be read as either 15 or 75). It would be worth a brief reminder that clear, unambiguous numerals, particularly 1s and 7s, are as important as the mathematics itself when working under time pressure.
