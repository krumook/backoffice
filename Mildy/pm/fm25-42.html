Q16(c) worked through — average speed vs acceleration

Question: work out the average speed of the cyclist between 15 seconds and 30 seconds.
What was written: −1.6 m/s, calculated as (0 − 8) ÷ (30 − 25) — this is actually correct as a calculation, just for the wrong quantity. It's the acceleration during the braking phase from 25s to 30s (change in speed ÷ time for that change), not the average speed over the full 15–30s interval.
The key distinction: average speed is always total distance travelled ÷ total time taken, never a difference of speeds. It also can never be negative, since distance travelled and time taken are both positive — a negative "average speed" is a useful warning sign that the wrong quantities were combined.
Step 1 — split 15s to 30s at the graph's shape change (25s): speed is constant at 8 m/s from 15s to 25s, then decreases in a straight line from 8 m/s to 0 m/s between 25s and 30s.
Step 2 — distance from 15s to 25s (constant speed): distance = speed × time = 8 × 10 = 80 m.
Step 3 — distance from 25s to 30s (constant deceleration, so use the trapezium/triangle area under the graph): distance = average of (8 and 0) × time = ((8+0)/2) × 5 = 20 m.
Step 4 — total distance and total time: total distance = 80 + 20 = 100 m; total time = 30 − 15 = 15 s.
Step 5 — average speed: 100 ÷ 15 = 6.67 m/s (matches the mark scheme's 6.666 to 6.733 range, which allows a small tolerance for reading the graph).

Q23 worked through — finding the segment angle instead of assuming it

Question: the diagram shows the major segment of a circle, centre O, radius 2.5m, which is the cross section of a tunnel with height 3m. The tunnel is 800m long. Calculate the volume.
What went wrong: the working used 60° as the angle at the centre without it being given anywhere in the question — the diagram is explicitly labelled "NOT TO SCALE", which is the exam's signal that any angle or length that looks a certain way in the picture cannot be read off or assumed, only calculated from the numbers actually given (height 3m, radius 2.5m).
Step 1 — find the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord: the height of 3m is measured from the chord to the top of the major segment. Since the full circle's diameter reach from the centre to the top is one radius (2.5m), and the tunnel height (3m) is more than the radius, the chord must sit below the centre, at distance (3 − 2.5) = 0.5m below O.
Step 2 — use that 0.5m with the radius to find the half-angle: in the right-angled triangle formed by the centre, the midpoint of the chord, and one end of the chord, the side adjacent to the half-angle is 0.5m and the hypotenuse is the radius, 2.5m. So cos(half-angle) = 0.5/2.5 = 0.2, giving half-angle = cos⁻¹(0.2) ≈ 78.46°.
Step 3 — double it for the full angle of the minor segment: angle = 2 × 78.46° ≈ 156.9°. (The reflex angle for the major segment, which is what the tunnel's cross section actually is, is 360° − 156.9° ≈ 203.1°.)
Step 4 — area of the major segment = area of circle − area of the minor segment, or equivalently, major sector area + triangle area (the triangle gets added back for a major segment, not subtracted): area ≈ 12.29 to 12.31 m² (matching the mark scheme).
Step 5 — volume of the tunnel = cross-sectional area × length = 12.3 × 800 ≈ 9835 to 9844 m³.
The one-line takeaway: whenever a diagram says "NOT TO SCALE" and an angle isn't printed on it, that angle must come from a calculation using the numbers given — never from how the picture looks.

Topics to target next session

NOT TO SCALE diagrams: calculate, don't assume

Q23's 60° assumption (worth 7 marks) is the single biggest loss on this paper, and it's a general habit rather than a one-off: any angle or length not printed on a "not to scale" diagram needs to be derived from the given numbers. Worth a few quick drills spotting "what's actually given vs. what's just drawn" before diving into the maths.

Scale drawings and bearings on screen

Q8 (5 marks) and Q17a/17c (6 marks) were all left blank, and both involve either measuring a printed distance or constructing with compasses/protractor — neither works well on a tablet screen. Worth redoing these specifically on a printed copy before treating them as a knowledge gap; if she can do them on paper, this isn't really a maths issue at all.

Carrying working from one line to the next

Q18b (sign flipped mid-expansion) and Q21 (a digit dropped copying the final answer) both had fully correct method but lost marks purely in the transcription between steps. A brief final check — re-reading the last line of working against the box just written — would likely catch both.

Describing a transformation from a diagram

Q10b was left blank despite both individual transformations (10a i and ii) being drawn correctly — the gap is specifically in going backwards from "here are two shapes" to naming the single transformation between them. A short, dedicated set of "describe the transformation" questions (not "perform the transformation") would target this directly.
