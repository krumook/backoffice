Weak topics to target next session

Bearings: the back-bearing rule

Question 4(b) shows a specific rule gap: the bearing of D from C should be found by adding 180° to the given bearing of C from D (130°+180°=310°), but 360°−130°=230 was used instead — a rule for a different bearing situation entirely. Worth a few dedicated back-bearing questions with a quick sketch each time to fix the "add or subtract 180°" rule.

Comparing magnitudes across different forms

Both Question 8 (percentages/fractions of m) and Question 11 (standard form) show the same underlying pattern: the actual size of a value isn’t always obvious from how "big" it looks written down. In Q8 the order was fully reversed (320% of m/10 looks large but is actually smallest); in Q11, 9×10¹&sup9; was picked as largest over the true largest, 1.3×10¹², because the exponents weren’t compared first. A few side-by-side comparison drills, converting everything to the same form before ordering, would help with both at once.

Applying a rounding rule to every quantity

In Question 25, the ±0.5 bound rule was applied correctly to the two cut pieces but not to the original 68cm measurement, which was treated as an exact value instead of a range. The individual pieces were bounded correctly — the gap is remembering that every rounded quantity in a question needs its own bounds, not just the ones that look like they need it.

Left blank under time pressure

Six separate answers (Q5b, the final step of Q12, Q19e, Q21, Q23b, Q26b) were left blank or unfinished — together the single biggest source of lost marks on this paper (12 of 26). Several were quick, low-mark questions with a short final step (Q12, Q19e) that seem to have been skipped rather than genuinely not understood. Worth practising a habit of always writing a final answer, even a best guess, before moving on.

Similar shapes: scale factor cubed for volume

Question 22(b) correctly found the linear scale factor from the surface areas (2:3) but didn’t correctly cube it to get the volume/capacity scale factor (2³:3³=8:27) and apply it to 56ml. A couple more area-to-volume similar-solids questions, explicitly labelling each step (linear → area → volume), would help make the cubing step automatic.
