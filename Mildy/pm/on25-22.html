11aE4.4Similarity / congruence0/3Misconception
Key ideas for similar shapes: corresponding angles are equal (every matching angle is the same size between the two shapes), and corresponding sides are proportional (every matching side length is linked by the same ratio).
The four points given — "ST and QR are parallel," "they have the same point of P," "S is a point on QP," "T is a point on RP" — are all true, but they're facts the question already gave, not reasons the triangles are similar. Since no side lengths are given before part (a), the proof has to run through angles:
Angle QPR = angle TPS (common angle).
Angle PQR = angle PST (corresponding, ST ∥ QR).
Angle PRQ = angle PTS (corresponding, ST ∥ QR).
Three pairs of equal corresponding angles (AAA) is what proves the triangles are similar.

23bE5.4Surface area and volume0/6Misconception
The method used (cos(60°) = a12, then Pythagoras inside that flat triangle) is a real, correctly-carried-out calculation — just for the wrong shape. It treats the sector's own chord and apothem as if they were the cone's radius and height.
When a sector is rolled into a cone, the arc length becomes the circumference of the base circle — that's the link that has to be used:
Arc length (part a) = 16πcm = base circumference → 2πr=16π → r=8
The sector's radius, 12cm, becomes the cone's slant height → 12²=8²+h² → h=√80
Volume = 13×π×8²×√80 = 256√53π cm³
Worth cutting out a sector and physically rolling it into a cone, so it's clear which measurement becomes which.

Weak topics to target next session

Answer the exact angle the question names, not just a correct one

Q6(b) and Q6(c) both show the right intermediate angle appearing in the working (AEC=100° correctly found; the isosceles base angle correctly found as 50°) but a different, related angle gets written in the final answer box instead of the one the question actually asked for. This is the single clearest repeating pattern on this paper — worth a habit of underlining the exact letters of the angle being asked for before writing a final answer.

Q17(a) — gradient was calculated instead of length (5 marks)

The gradient of BD was found twice, correctly, but the question needed BD's length (via the distance formula) to apply the 2:3 ratio and find AC. The two calculations — "how steep" vs "how long" — seem to be merging into one in her mind. Worth a side-by-side drill: given two coordinates, find the gradient AND the length as two separate labelled answers.

Q23(b) — folding a sector into a cone (6 marks, the single biggest loss)

The method used (cos(60)=a/12, then Pythagoras within the flat sector triangle) is a real, coherent calculation — just for the wrong shape. Turning a sector into a cone specifically requires matching the sector's arc length to the cone's base circumference (2πr), not using the sector's own internal chord/apothem geometry. Worth building this physically — rolling a paper sector into an actual cone — before the next question of this type.

Q11(a) — a similarity proof needs angle equalities, not restated facts

All four answers given ("ST and QR are parallel," "same point of P," "S is a point on QP," "T is a point on RP") repeat information already stated in the question rather than proving similarity. With no side lengths given, the only route is through angles: one shared angle plus two pairs of equal corresponding angles (from the parallel lines). Worth a few practice proofs that specifically forbid restating the question's own setup as a "reason."

Two blanks on topics not yet taught

Q13(b) (finding R so that M/R is a cube number, via prime factorisation) and Q22 (completing the square in reverse, given a minimum point) were both left blank. Neither looks like a slip or a forgotten method — both are new sub-skills that haven't had a dedicated lesson yet, worth scheduling short, focused introductions for each rather than treating them as gaps in known material.
