Weak topics to target next session

Reading exactly what's being asked, before writing the final answer

Three separate losses this paper came from computing something real and correct, then writing down the wrong quantity as the final answer: total balance instead of total interest (Q3b), multiplying instead of dividing for a unit cost (Q4d(ii)(a)), and using 1m² instead of the given 2.5m² (Q4d(ii)(b)). The maths itself wasn't the problem in any of these — a habit of re-reading the question's exact final ask right before writing the answer line would catch all three.

Matching a formula to the actual shape described

Two mensuration losses came from applying a formula that didn't match the situation: filling water to the full height of the large cube instead of just to the smaller cube's top (Q4b), and using the right-angled-triangle area formula (½×base×height) for an equilateral triangle cross-section (Q4c). Worth a couple of questions where she has to first identify which formula actually applies before calculating anything.

Following a named method when the question specifies one

Question 7(a)(ii)(b) explicitly asked to solve "by factorisation," but the quadratic formula was used instead. The final answer was right, so only the method marks were lost — a useful reminder to scan for a named technique in the question before starting, since a different (even successful) method usually doesn't earn those marks.

Careful substitution into memorised formulas

Both Q6c (quadratic formula) and Q10b(ii) (perpendicular line through a point) had the right values identified correctly on paper, but a substitution or multiplication slip crept in when actually plugging them into the formula. Slowing down specifically at the substitution step — writing each value into its exact slot before simplifying — would catch this pattern.
