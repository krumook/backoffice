22cE2.12Range of values for fewer solutions0/2 Left blank
Left blank — the key idea: y = k is a horizontal line, and the number of solutions is how many times it crosses the curve.
From part (a), the stationary points are (−1, 1) and (1/3, −5/27).
Between these values the line crosses the curve 3 times; outside them it only crosses once.
So: k > 1 or k < −5/27.
Good question to think about: what happens exactly at the boundary? There the line just touches the curve at the stationary point, giving a repeated root — only 2 distinct solutions, not 3. So k = 1 and k = −5/27 also count as "fewer than 3" — but Cambridge's mark scheme still asks for the strict version above as the expected answer.

23bE2.4Solving an exponential equation2/4 Misconception
Good start — 4x+3 was correctly rewritten as 22(x+3), and a linear equation was set up from the powers.
The fix: rewrite every term in base 2 individually first, rather than combining terms early.
16x = 24x,  (½)x = 2−x,  4x+3 = 22x+6
24x × 2−x = 22x+6 → 23x = 22x+6
3x = 2x+6 → x = 6
Instead, 16x and (½)x were combined directly into 82x (it should be 8x) — an extra factor of 2 crept into the exponent, and the working also wasn't carried through to a final numerical answer.

Topics to target next session

Handwriting: the digit 4

Three answers (Q5, Q7, Q8a) were mathematically correct but hard to mark at first glance, and two of them (Q7, Q8a) trace back to the same cause: a 4 written with a closed top that reads as a 9. Combined with the 1/7 confusion noted on the previous paper, it's worth a short, deliberate focus on writing numerals in a consistently open, unambiguous style, particularly under time pressure.

Combining indices before converting

In Q23(b), 16x × (1/2)x was converted to 82x instead of 8x. Converting every term to the same base individually first (as in the guideline above), rather than combining bases part-way through, would remove this source of error.

Carrying working through to a final answer

Q22(c) was left blank and Q23(b) stopped short of a final numerical answer, despite correct or largely correct working already in place for both. This matches the pattern seen on the previous paper: confidence can drop before a question is finished. Encouraging a tentative final answer to be written down even when unsure, rather than leaving the line blank, should recover marks directly.
