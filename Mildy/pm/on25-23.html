Weak topics to target next session

Q2 — largest odd common factor (1 mark)

The division-ladder method was on the right track, but dividing by 10 first (not a prime number) muddies the process, and the final answer written down (9) was actually just an intermediate remainder from partway through the ladder, not even the full common factor. Worth practising the ladder method with primes only, and treating "largest odd" as its own extra check at the end — strip out any factor of 2 before finishing.

Q24(b) — area of a rectangle with surd sides (2 marks)

The harder skill (simplifying 3√8 down to 6√2) was done correctly — the miss was adding the two side lengths instead of multiplying them for area. Worth a quick standing check on any "find the area" question: read the word "area" and say "multiply" out loud before starting, especially when the numbers involved are surds and there's more to think about already.

Q14(a)/(b) — vertex form of a quadratic, not yet taught (2 marks)

Given a turning point, finding the line of symmetry and writing the equation in vertex form y=(x+a)²+b is a new sub-skill that hasn't had a dedicated lesson yet — this is the same gap flagged on Paper 22's Q22, so worth prioritising a short, focused lesson on it before it appears a third time.

Q17(b) — reflex angle from a special cosine value (2 marks)

The harder half of this question — recalling that cos45°=1/√2 — was worked out correctly and is visible in the working. What's missing is the last conversion step: turning that 45° reference angle into the actual reflex angle the question asked for (315°). Worth a quick drill on reflex/obtuse angle conversions once the reference angle is already known, since that's specifically where this one stalled.
