Q2 worked through — finishing the isosceles triangle

Question: in the diagram, AC=BC. A ray extends beyond C, forming 148° and 82° with CB and CA respectively. Work out angle CAB.
What was written: 130° — this is angle ACB, not angle CAB. It's a correct intermediate answer given the wrong final label.
Step 1 — find angle ACB using angles around a point: the three angles at C (148°, 82°, and angle ACB) sit all the way around point C, so they sum to 360°: angle ACB = 360−148−82 = 130°.
Step 2 — recognise which triangle rule applies now: AC=BC means triangle ABC is isosceles, with the two equal sides meeting at C. The angle between the two equal sides (130°) is the apex angle; the two base angles, at A and at B, are equal to each other and are what's left over from 180°.
Step 3 — find the base angle: angle CAB = angle CBA = (180−130)÷2 = 25°.
The one-line takeaway: when a question names two specific letters for an angle (CAB, not just “the angle at C”), double-check which vertex that's centred on before writing the final answer — especially in an isosceles-triangle question, where the apex angle and base angle are almost never the same number.

Q24 worked through — similar triangles give the ratio, not a direct copy

Question: O is the origin, OA=a, OB=b. OBD and ACD are straight lines. BC=⅓OA. M is the midpoint of CD. Find OM in terms of a and b.
What went wrong: BD was taken to be ⅓b, copying the ⅓ straight from BC=⅓OA. But BC and BD are different segments in a different triangle relationship — the ⅓ from BC=⅓OA doesn't automatically transfer onto BD.
Step 1 — notice BC is parallel to OA: BC=⅓OA means BC is a scaled copy of OA pointing the same way, so BC ∥ OA.
Step 2 — use that parallel to set up similar triangles: triangles DBC and DOA share the angle at D, and BC ∥ OA means the other two angles match too (corresponding angles) — so triangle DBC is similar to triangle DOA, with the same 1:3 scale factor as BC:OA.
Step 3 — read the ratio off the correct pair of sides: in similar triangles, matching sides are in the same ratio, so DB:DO = BC:OA = 1:3. That means DB is ⅓ of DO (not of OB) — so DO = 3×DB, and OB = DO−DB = 3DB−DB = 2DB, giving DB=½OB=½b.
Step 4 — carry that through to OC and OD: OD = OB+BD = b+½b = &frac32;b. OC = OB+BC = b+⅓a.
Step 5 — find CD, then CM, then OM: CD = OD−OC = &frac32;b−b−⅓a = ½b−⅓a. CM = ½CD = ¼b−Ῑa. OM = OC+CM = (b+⅓a) + (¼b−Ῑa) = ⅙a + &frac54;b.
The one-line takeaway: a ratio from one pair of similar-triangle sides (BC:OA) only transfers directly onto a different segment (BD) if you can show it's the same corresponding pair — here BD corresponds to DO, not to OB, so the working step (DB:DO=1:3) has to come first.

Topics to target next session

Similar triangles: match the correct pair of sides

Q24's single biggest loss (3 marks) came from applying BC's ratio to OA directly onto BD, rather than working out which sides in the similar triangles actually correspond. Worth a few more “show that BD=...” style questions where the ratio isn't the one that's given directly.

Answering the exact quantity asked

Q2 found the right number (130°) but for the wrong angle — a pattern that also showed up on FebMarch2025 P42 (Q16c: average speed vs acceleration). Before writing a final answer, it's worth re-reading the question's exact wording — which letters, which quantity — one more time.

Tree diagrams: keep each bag's own numbers on its own branches

Q20(b)(i) copied Bag A's probabilities onto Bag B's branches, even though Q20(c) later shows she handles Bag B's real contents (3 white, 1 black) correctly when it's the main focus of a question. Worth double-checking, on any two-stage tree, that each set of branches uses that stage's own totals.

Q5 — both parts lost, for different reasons

5(a) used the generic word “transformation” instead of “translation” and read the vector's sign backwards; 5(b) (the enlargement) was left blank entirely. Worth revisiting the vocabulary for all four transformation types side by side, and making sure every part of a multi-part question gets at least an attempt.

Left blank this paper

Q8 (simultaneous equations, 5 marks) and Q19 (exponential equation, 3 marks) were both completely blank — no working at all, unlike most other gaps on this paper which at least show an attempt. Worth finding out directly whether this was time pressure (both are later in the paper) or not knowing where to start, since the two need different fixes.
