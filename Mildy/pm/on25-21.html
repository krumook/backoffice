Topics to target next session

Similar triangles: match the correct pair of sides

Q24's single biggest loss (3 marks) came from applying BC's ratio to OA directly onto BD, rather than working out which sides in the similar triangles actually correspond. Worth a few more “show that BD=...” style questions where the ratio isn't the one that's given directly.

Answering the exact quantity asked

Q2 found the right number (130°) but for the wrong angle — a pattern that also showed up on FebMarch2025 P42 (Q16c: average speed vs acceleration). Before writing a final answer, it's worth re-reading the question's exact wording — which letters, which quantity — one more time.

Tree diagrams: keep each bag's own numbers on its own branches

Q20(b)(i) copied Bag A's probabilities onto Bag B's branches, even though Q20(c) later shows she handles Bag B's real contents (3 white, 1 black) correctly when it's the main focus of a question. Worth double-checking, on any two-stage tree, that each set of branches uses that stage's own totals.

Q5 — both parts lost, for different reasons

5(a) used the generic word “transformation” instead of “translation” and read the vector's sign backwards; 5(b) (the enlargement) was left blank entirely. Worth revisiting the vocabulary for all four transformation types side by side, and making sure every part of a multi-part question gets at least an attempt.

Left blank this paper

Q8 (simultaneous equations, 5 marks) and Q19 (exponential equation, 3 marks) were both completely blank — no working at all, unlike most other gaps on this paper which at least show an attempt. Worth finding out directly whether this was time pressure (both are later in the paper) or not knowing where to start, since the two need different fixes.
