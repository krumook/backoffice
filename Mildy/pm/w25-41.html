27E6.6Pythagoras in 3D: angle between a diagonal and a base1/4Misconception
This question asks for the angle between the diagonal AG and the base plane of a trapezoidal prism. Marking angle θ correctly at A on the diagram earns the one method mark available for that step. The calculation that followed assumes a right angle at F that the shape doesn't actually have:
tanθ = √345√65 ⇒ θ ≈ 17.9°  ←  assumes a 90° angle at F
Checking with the dot product of the two edges meeting at F shows they aren't actually perpendicular, so the triangle used for that tan ratio isn't a valid right-angled triangle in the first place. The correct route projects G straight down onto the base to get the true horizontal run, then uses that in the tangent ratio:
horizontal run = √(15²+7²) = √274
tanθ = 5√274 ⇒ θ ≈ 16.8°
This is a genuinely hard 3D-geometry setup — correctly identifying and marking the angle itself is a real step in the right direction, but the triangle chosen to calculate it from wasn't a valid one for this shape. Worth working through together with a physical or drawn 3D sketch, checking each face's actual angles rather than assuming one looks like 90°.

Weak topics to target next session

Vector and vocabulary direction: OA vs AO

Q10 wrote AO where OA was needed — the two vectors are exact opposites, so every component flips sign. Worth a standing habit of saying the direction out loud ("from O to A") before writing any position vector, since the two labels look nearly identical on the page.

Angle of elevation/depression: observer height matters

Q13 used an object's full height directly in the tan ratio instead of subtracting the observer's 1.5m eye height first. This is a specific, nameable trap worth flagging directly — whenever a question gives an observer's own height, check whether it needs subtracting before setting up the right-angled triangle.

Sine rule: pairing each side with the angle actually opposite it

Q29b paired side AD with 45° instead of the 38° that's actually opposite it in the triangle, dropping 3 marks even though everything downstream (the cosine rule step, using her own AD) was carried out correctly. Worth a quick double-check habit: before writing sin(angle)/side, trace the triangle to confirm the two are actually opposite each other.

Worth pointing out explicitly next time: read every part of a construction question separately before skipping the whole thing.

Carrying values correctly from one part (or line) to the next

Q14b dropped π partway through a frustum-volume expression, Q14c's mass calculation was exactly right but never converted from grams to the kilograms the question asked for, and Q14d used a mismatched volume value (2533π) instead of correctly reusing the volume she'd already found in part (a). All three are the same underlying habit within one multi-part question — re-write the full expression (including constants and units) at the start of each new line, and copy values straight from an earlier boxed answer rather than re-deriving or half-remembering them.
